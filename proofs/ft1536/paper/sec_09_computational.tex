\subsection{An admitted class of randomized tests}
For a finite tape space $X$ and law $\tau$, a \code{CompTest} is a
function $t:X\to[0,1]$. The operative premise is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comp}
 \code{CompPRGBound}(\mathcal C,\tau,\dprg)
 \iff\forall t\in\mathcal C,\quad
 \left|\sum_x(\tau(x)-\U_X(x))t(x)\right|\leq\dprg.
\end{equation}
The admitted class $\mathcal C$ and a cost function are parameters.
Their names do not establish efficient implementability; that must
be supplied by the intended instantiation.\src{C15}

Given a continuation $F:X\to\code{Dist}(Y)$ and an event $E$, the
winning test is $t_F(x)=\Pr[F(x)\in E]$. It averages the randomness
remaining after the tape has been fixed. For the public stream game,
that randomness includes the public key, adversary coins, nonces and
fresh ROM replies; adaptive messages remain inside the continuation.
It is generally not a deterministic indicator on $X$.\src{C15}

\begin{proposition}[Restricted-test game hop]\label{prop:hop}
If \eqref{eq:comp} holds and $t_F\in\mathcal C$, then
\[
 \left|\Pr_{x\leftarrow\tau}[F(x)\in E]
       -\Pr_{x\leftarrow\U_X}[F(x)\in E]\right|\leq\dprg.
\]
\end{proposition}
Proposition~\ref{prop:hop} is \code{comp_game_hop_abs}. Its proof rewrites both probabilities
as finite expectations and applies \eqref{eq:comp} to that test.
It does not pass through a total-variation bound. In contrast, the
older predicate named \code{ChaCha20PRFBound} quantifies over all
events and is proved equivalent to a total-variation bound. Its
name and comments do not make its quantifier computational.
The finite-state theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:routea}) explains why
that unrestricted reading cannot give a small error here.\src{C10,C15}

\subsection{One tape for the whole run}
The stream experiment draws one tape in
$X=\{0,1\}^{q_s S.\mathrm{bits}}$, not an independent real-generator
tape at every adaptive query. \code{windowOf} selects a query window;
\code{windowsOf} and \code{flattenWindows} are inverse maps between
the whole tape and its table of windows. Uniformity transports through
this equivalence, including zero-width and zero-query cases.
The induction \code{simulateStream_uniform} handles the adaptive
continuations and yields
\[
 P_\U=\Pr[\code{lazyGame}=\mathrm{true}]
      =\Adv_{\mathrm{MT}}(q_h+1,\mu_H,B_A).
\]
This identity belongs to the public simulator. It does not identify
a stream-driven honest signer with \code{Games.runEUF}.\src{C15}

The exact modeled consumption names are
\[
 N=q_s S.\mathrm{bits},\qquad
 b_{\mathrm{bytes}}=(N+7)\mathbin{\mathrm{div}}8,\qquad
 q_{\mathrm{blocks}}=(b_{\mathrm{bytes}}+63)\mathbin{\mathrm{div}}64.
\]
The modeled generator has $64$-byte blocks and a $4096$-byte refill
buffer. The formal nonce draw is $320$ bits per signing query and is
outside this ChaCha tape account. These definitions do not prove that
the C signer's concatenated, potentially reinitialized instances have
the selected joint tape law; seeding, dependence and addressing remain
source/operational obligations.\src{C10,C15}

\subsection{The cost and key-law premises must be consumed}
For the actual winning test $t$, the certificate has two fields:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cost}
 \code{CompWinCert}:\qquad t\in\mathcal C,\qquad
 \operatorname{cost}(t)\leq T_A+q_{\mathrm{blocks}}c_{\mathrm{block}}.
\end{equation}
The stream hop takes this full certificate. Its membership field feeds
the probability argument, and its cost field is returned as a
conjunct of the exported result. An unconstrained class-membership
assertion alone cannot provide the second field. No machine-cost
semantics is manufactured by the inequality.\src{C15}

The computational assembly also names two laws on secret/public-key
pairs, $\mu_E$ and $\mu_K$. The stream test uses
$\code{muH}(\mu_E)$ and the MT experiment uses $\code{muH}(\mu_K)$.
Its interface is
\[
 \code{KeyLawBinding}(K,\mu_E,\mu_K):\qquad K\Longrightarrow\mu_E=\mu_K,
\]
together with a proof of $K$. The equality is actually used in the
export. Taking $K=\mathrm{True}$ still requires that equality; it
cannot identify arbitrary different laws. Naming a law ``emitted''
does not prove its source provenance.\src{C15}

\begin{theorem}[Corrected public-simulation assembly]\label{thm:public}
For the finite types, budget, adversary, sampler and full-run tape law
above, assume \code{CompPRGBound}, the full certificate
\eqref{eq:cost} for \code{streamWinTest} under $\code{muH}(\mu_E)$,
and the key-law binding with a proof of its proposition. Then
\[
 P_\tau\leq b_K+\dprg
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \operatorname{cost}(t)\leq T_A+q_{\mathrm{blocks}}c_{\mathrm{block}},
\]
where $b_K=\Adv_{\mathrm{MT}}(q_h+1,\code{muH}(\mu_K),B_A)$.
If $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{stream}}\leq P_\tau$, the corresponding lower
bound is $\max\{0,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{stream}}-\dprg\}\leq b_K$,
with the same cost conjunct.
\end{theorem}
These are \code{public_sim_stream_bound} and
\code{advMT_ge_of_public_sim_stream_win}. No sampler-divergence
parameter is needed for this experiment. The separate $\Phi$-weakening
exports admit arbitrary $D\geq0$; their inverse lower bound is
dominated by the clipped direct bound when $\epscoll\geq0$.
The clipping at zero is necessary.\src{C15,C16}

The review process exposed three contract defects in earlier exports:
the experiment was mislabeled as honest Sign, the cost certificate
was not consumed, and the key proposition was unused. The corrected
types address those defects for the public experiment. REVIEW\_002
is the author's recheck; the later independent REVIEW\_003 records
\code{PASS_SCOPED} for the corrected package. The honest continuation,
its own winning test and its fair-law identity remain the first two
arrows of Section~\ref{sec:main}.\src{C17}
