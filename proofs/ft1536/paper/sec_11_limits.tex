\subsection{Support, error shape and resources}
The byte bridge has a mathematical consequence, not merely a
serialization consequence. If a candidate reply law assigns nonzero
mass to a positive output having no norm-admissible preimage under
\code{emit} on the relevant fiber, then
\code{chi2_top_of_out_of_support} gives $\chi^2=+\infty$.
A small additive probability estimate cannot remove that support
failure (cf. Theorem~\ref{thm:additive}). Conversely, wrap-related verifier rejection within the
modeled emit image does not automatically cause failure of absolute
continuity. The support theorem states which case is being used.
\src{C19}

The additive-to-multiplicative machine lemma is closed only with its
pointwise floor and error-budget premises. The numeric scalar-leaf
floor does not discharge a floor on arbitrary weights. Similarly,
the $2^{-32}$ and $2^{-17}$ bounds attach to different attempt shapes
(Remark~\ref{rmk:shape});
the latter cannot be hidden by labeling the former a universal sampler
constant. The final source realization must settle that distinction.
\src{C08,C19}

The formal probability reduction gives a specified reducer, while the
computational public-game export (Theorem~\ref{thm:public}) carries a
parametric cost contract.
Neither statement alone proves a concrete runtime class for the full
real-world reduction. Nor does the table of security proxies evaluate
the complete query-dependent bound: the amplification
$(1+e)^{q_s}-1$, collision loss and PRG term remain explicit.
\src{C04,C14,C15}

\subsection{Conclusion}
The present contribution is a conditional classical reduction, computed
sampler-comparison margins, explicit impossibility results for insufficient
premises, and source-binding work that has found both C and model defects.
The remaining work is a set of named implementation and law interfaces,
not an implicit consequence of the theorem names. The intended result
is independently inspectable cryptographic software for every free person.
