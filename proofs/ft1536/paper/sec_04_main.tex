\subsection{The four-arrow target}
To expose the two unfinished interfaces, split the public-simulation
identification into its existing stream and lazy-game components:
\begin{align*}
 \text{real Sign on real randomness}
   &\xrightarrow{\;1\;}\text{same Sign on fair randomness}\\
   &\xrightarrow{\;2\;}\text{public fair-tape simulation}\\
   &\xrightarrow{\;3\;}\text{public lazy game}\\
   &\xrightarrow{\;4\;}\text{MT-ISIS with }\code{Reduction.build}.
\end{align*}
Arrow 1 is a computational game hop for an honest signer. Arrow 2
requires law identification and the directional second-moment
certificate, with stopping loss. Arrows 3 and 4 are the existing
\code{streamGame_uniform} and \code{concrete_lazy_game_binding}
identifications. Arrow 2 denotes an advantage comparison, not equality
of the honest and public laws.\src{C01,C04,C05,C15}

\begin{target}[Intended real-signing statement]\label{target:main}
Let $B_A$ be the reducer \code{Reduction.build beta A S}, and let
$b=\Adv_{\mathrm{MT}}(q_h+1,\mu_H,B_A)$. After arrows 1--2 and their
law interfaces have been supplied, the target is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
 \varepsilon_{\mathrm{real}}
 \leq \min\{1,\epscoll+\Phi((1+e)^{q_s}-1,b)\}+\dprg,
 \qquad e=k^{32}-1.
\end{equation}
Here $k$ is the realized attempt factor, $\dprg$ bounds the admitted
honest winning test at the stated randomness horizon, and the
unconditioned candidate factor has $e<2^{-32}$. The acceptance-conditioned
alternative is stated in Remark~\ref{rmk:shape}.
\end{target}
This is a target, not a proved theorem about C in this draft.
In particular, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{real}}$ is a target experiment name,
not an alias for the public-simulation advantage.\src{C01,C06,C08}

\subsection{The closed conditional theorem}
The actual honest-game theorem requires the following local data:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cert}
 \begin{gathered}
 \code{LocalJointCertificate S e}:\quad e\geq0,\\
 \forall h,st,m,r:\quad
  \AC(j_{h,st,m,r},p_h),\qquad
  \Second(j_{h,st,m,r},p_h)\leq1+e,\\
 j_{h,st,m,r}=\U_{\{0,1\}^{S.\mathrm{bits}}}\mathbin{.}\mathrm{map}
       (S.\mathrm{code}\ h\ st\ m\ r),\qquad
 p_h=\code{SigmaMath.freshHonest}\ h.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional classical reduction]\label{thm:conditional}
For any finite secret-key type, budget $\beta$, key law $\mu_K$, classical
adversary $A$, public sampler $S$ and real $e$, a certificate
\eqref{eq:cert} implies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional}
 \Adv_{\mathrm{EUF}}(\beta,\mu_K,A)
 \leq \min\{1,\epscoll+\Phi((1+e)^{q_s}-1,b)\},
\end{equation}
where $\mu_H=\code{SigmaMath.muH}\ \mu_K$, $B_A$ is the constructor
above, and
\begin{align}
 \epscoll&=\min\left\{1,
 \frac{q_s q_h+q_s(q_s-1)/2}{2^{320}}\right\},\label{eq:coll}\\
 \Phi(D,b)&=\frac{2b+D+\sqrt{D^2+4Db(1-b)}}{2(1+D)}.
 \label{eq:phi}
\end{align}
The subtraction $q_s-1$ in \eqref{eq:coll} is truncated natural
subtraction, as in the Lean declaration.
\end{theorem}
This is \code{Run2.concrete_euf_cma_to_mt_isis}, with
\code{exact_collision_parameter}. Its quantifiers supply a concrete
reducer for each $A,S$; they do not supply an implementation instance of
$S$, the certificate or a machine-resource bound.\src{C04}

The separate corrected \code{AssemblyComp.public_sim_stream_bound}
concerns the public experiment $P_\tau$, and yields
$P_\tau\leq b+\dprg$ with the test-cost conjunct. It is not a proof of
\eqref{eq:target}. The older \code{Assembly} export adds a nonnegative
statistical term to \eqref{eq:conditional}; inspection of its proof
also shows that its proposition-valued \code{hkey} is discarded.
Its end-to-end name therefore supplies neither missing source binding.
The corrected computational consumer uses a dependent equality between
the two named key laws instead.\src{C01,C15}

\subsection{Exact inverse and the scope test}
\begin{theorem}[Generalized inverse]\label{thm:inverse}
For $D\geq0$ and $a\leq1$, the formal inverse has the ordinary-real form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:psi}
 \psi_D(a)=\begin{cases}
 0,& a\leq D/(1+D),\\
 a-\sqrt{Da(1-a)},&D/(1+D)<a\leq1.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
For $0\leq b\leq1$, $a\leq\Phi(D,b)$ implies $\psi_D(a)\leq b$;
moreover $\psi_D(\Phi(D,b))=b$.
\end{theorem}
These are \code{phiInv_piecewise}, \code{phi_inv_le} and
\code{phiInv_at_phi}. The active branch has a nonnegative radicand;
the lower branch includes negative $a$. Lean defines the total function
as $\max\{0,a-\operatorname{Real.sqrt}(Da(1-a))\}$, with its total
square-root convention. No inverse assertion for $a>1$ is used.
Tiny positive $a$ below the threshold give zero, not a positive
MT lower bound.\src{C16}

Substituting $k=1$ makes $D=(1+(k^{32}-1))^{q_s}-1=0$, and
$\Phi(0,b)=b$. The corrected public-simulation weakening allows this
choice for arbitrary $S$ because its $D$ is only a weakening parameter.
That scope test cannot establish a real-sampler certificate. A future
assembly must demonstrate actual consumption of \eqref{eq:cert}, rather
than merely contain a $\Phi$ expression.\src{C16,C17}
