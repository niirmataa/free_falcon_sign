\section{The classical reduction}\label{sec:reduction}
\status{\CLOSED{} conditional reduction}
This section presents the closed reduction as a self-contained
mathematical argument. Its objects are the laws of
Section~\ref{sec:model}, its conclusion is
Theorem~\ref{thm:conditional}, and each step is written out so that it
can be read without running a proof assistant: the direction of the
second moment (Section~\ref{sec:direction}), the induction over the
adaptive adversary (Lemma~\ref{lem:composition}), the collision loss
(Section~\ref{sec:collstop}) and the extraction into the MT-ISIS
experiment (Section~\ref{sec:extraction}). The formal exports that
carry the same steps are listed in the compliance table of
Appendix~\ref{app:compliance}.\src{C04,C05}

\subsection{One honest reply, including failure}
The finite candidate space \code{BoxPair} contains two vectors with
coordinates in $[-65535,65535]$. This is an enclosing box, not a
centering domain. Every pair with $Q<B$ has a representation in it.
For the syndrome map $A_h$ and challenge $c$, the reference weight is
\[
 g_{h,c}(z)=\ind[A_h(\operatorname{decode}z)=c]\,
 \exp\!\left(-\frac{Q(\operatorname{decode}z)}{2\cdot768^2}\right).
\]
The reference trial normalizes these weights, maps a norm rejection to
$\none$, and returns $\none$ on an empty fiber. The honest body is
exactly
\[
 \code{signBody}(A_h,c)
 =\code{cap}(\code{trial}(A_h,c),16)\mathbin{.}\mathrm{map}(\code{emit}).
\]
The cap retries trial failure; emission occurs once after the cap and
returns the second vector only when it is signed-16. The model does
not perform another centering or Verify check on a positive emission.
This is a probability-law specification; an efficient exact sampler
is not inferred from its definition.\src{C05}

Retries and failures are part of this experiment, not remarks on it.
A norm rejection is a draw of the $\none$ mass and is retried, at most
$16$ times; cap exhaustion returns $\none$ and remains in the law as a
real outcome. Emission failure --- a positive trial whose second
vector is not signed-$16$ --- is terminal and is not retried. For any
one-attempt law $t$, let
$G_{16}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{15}t(\none)^i$ and let
$I_t(o)=\sum_{z:\,\mathrm{emit}(z)=o}t(\mathrm{some}\ z)$.
The exact failure accounting is
\begin{align*}
 \code{signBodyOf}(t)(\mathrm{some}\ v)&=G_{16}(t)I_t(\mathrm{some}\ v),\\
 \code{signBodyOf}(t)(\none)&=t(\none)^{16}+G_{16}(t)I_t(\none).
\end{align*}
Thus cap exhaustion and emission failure are both retained. A small
probability of verifier rejection after positive emission is not itself
a chi-square certificate.\src{C05,C06}

\subsection{Public simulation and extraction}\label{sec:extraction}
The sampler interface accepts only $h$, the public state, message,
nonce and a finite tape, and returns a pair $(c,o)$ of challenge and
reply. The simulator first records the submitted message and draws a
fresh nonce. If its hash name is occupied, it stops. Otherwise it runs
$S$, programs that name to the returned challenge, and records the
reply. The fresh honest law instead draws a uniform challenge and
uses its reference body. These two laws are compared, not identified.
\src{C05}

The reducer draws the adversary's coins and executes this simulator
against a fixed list of $q_h+1$ uniform targets. Fresh adversarial hash
queries consume target entries; signing-programmed entries carry no
target index. Final verification may consume one further target.
The submitted-message freshness check prevents a signing-programmed
entry from becoming a valid fresh-message forgery. For an accepted
forgery the reducer returns its recorded target index and
\code{Relation.extract}; output soundness checks the same relation as
the MT experiment, by the extraction of
Proposition~\ref{prop:extracts}.\src{C05}

Written out, the extraction argument has four steps.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A run of the public simulation against the fixed target list
 either stops at an occupied name or completes; on completion the
 table of programmed names is exactly the transcript of the adversary's
 fresh hash queries and of the simulator's own signings.
\item If the adversary ends with an accepted forgery $(m^\ast,s^\ast)$,
 the freshness gate guarantees that $m^\ast$ was never submitted as a
 message, so the name used at final verification is not
 signing-programmed and therefore consumed a target entry; the reducer
 returns its index $i\leq q_h+1$.
\item Acceptance means the coefficient predicate of
 Section~\ref{sec:model} holds for $(h,m^\ast,s^\ast)$; by
 Proposition~\ref{prop:extracts} the extracted pair is a short
 preimage for the corresponding target, which is exactly the success
 condition of Definition~\ref{def:mtisis}.
\item Consequently, a winning public-simulation run yields an MT-ISIS
 output with the same probability, and no premise about hidden
 execution traces is used: the identification of the two output laws
 is equality of expectations for output functions
 (\code{Dist.Same}).\src{C05}
\end{enumerate}
The resulting law identity is
\[
 \code{runMT}(q_h+1,\mu_H,B_A)
 \mathrel{\code{Dist.Same}}
 \code{lazyGame}(\beta,\mu_H,A,S).
\]
Here \code{Dist.Same} is equality of expectations for output functions;
it does not assert identical hidden execution traces. No independent
whole-game equality premise is inserted into
Theorem~\ref{thm:conditional}.\src{C05}

\subsection{The direction of the second moment}\label{sec:direction}
The certificate compares the \emph{simulated} reply law $j$ against the
\emph{honest} law $p$, in that direction:
$\Second(j,p)=1+\chi^2(j\Vert p)$ with $\AC(j,p)$
(Definition~\ref{def:second}). The direction is load-bearing in two
ways. The reduction must upper-bound the honest winning probability,
so the density ratio it needs is $j/p$, not $p/j$; and
$\AC(j,p)$ --- not its converse --- is the support condition that fails
exactly when the simulation emits outside the honest law's image
(Section~\ref{sec:limits}). A bound on $\Second(p,j)$ would control the
wrong ratio and proves nothing used here.\src{C03,C04}

The event-level consequence is the following computation. Fix a
winning event $E$, put $a=p(E)$, $b=j(E)$, and let $f=\ind[E]$. Under
$p$ the indicator has variance
\[
 \sum_x p(x)\,(f(x)-a)^2 = a(1-a),
\]
and with the density ratio $r(x)=j(x)/p(x)$ on the support of $p$,
\[
 b-a=\sum_x p(x)\,(r(x)-1)\,(f(x)-a).
\]
Weighted Cauchy--Schwarz applied to $r-1$ and $f-a$ then gives
\[
 (a-b)^2\ \leq\ \Bigl(\sum_x p(x)(r(x)-1)^2\Bigr)\cdot a(1-a)
 \ =\ \chi^2(j\Vert p)\cdot a(1-a),
\]
because $\sum_x p(x)(r(x)-1)^2 = \Second(j,p)-1$. Thus for every event,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quad}
 (a-b)^2\ \leq\ D\,a(1-a),\qquad D=\Second(j,p)-1.
\end{equation}
Solving \eqref{eq:quad} for $a$ --- the quadratic
$(1+D)a^2-(2b+D)a+b^2\leq 0$ --- keeps the larger root and yields
$a\leq\Phi(D,b)$ with $\Phi$ of \eqref{eq:phi}. This is why the honest
probability is bounded through the simulated one, and why the error
term enters as $D=(1+e)^{q_s}-1$ after the composition below. For
$D=0$ the inequality degenerates to $a\leq b$, the content of the
$k=1$ scope test (Corollary~\ref{cor:scope}).\src{C04,C16}

\subsection{Adaptive composition and stopping loss}
\begin{lemma}[Adaptive composition and stopping loss]\label{lem:composition}
The certificate compares the full $(c,o)$ law at every fresh signing
point, including $o=\none$, and consumes its absolute continuity and
second-moment fields. Induction on the adversary program then gives the
factor $(1+e)^{q_s}$: hash steps do not pay this factor, and a Sign step
pays it before following the adaptive continuation.
\end{lemma}
This is the content of \code{programComparison} and
\code{stoppedGameComparison}. The induction, written out, runs over the
adversary's program:\src{C04,C05}
\begin{itemize}
\item at a \emph{done} step both executions stop and the comparison is
 reflexive, with factor $1$;
\item at a \emph{hash} step both games answer from the same programmed
 record, so the reply laws are identical and the factor stays $1$;
\item at a \emph{sign} step the certificate compares the complete
 one-reply law of the honest body with the sampler law at the current
 public key, state, message and nonce, giving $\Second\leq 1+e$; the
 factor is multiplied in \emph{before} the adaptive continuation is
 fed the reply. The certificate is quantified at every public state and
 message, so nothing about the continuation has to be predictable in
 advance.
\end{itemize}
Second moments multiply under this composition whenever the absolute
continuity fields hold at each step, and the program type bounds the
number of Sign tokens by $q_s$; the product is therefore at most
$(1+e)^{q_s}$. The second-moment direction of
Section~\ref{sec:direction} then transfers the winning event with
$D=(1+e)^{q_s}-1$.\src{C04,C05}

\subsection{Removing the collision stop}\label{sec:collstop}
The stopped run halts when a drawn name is already occupied in the
table of hash names. Removing this stop changes the advantage by at
most the clipped parameter \eqref{eq:coll}, and the counting behind it
is elementary. Each of at most $q_s$ signing queries draws a fresh
uniform $40$-byte, $320$-bit nonce. A run can reach an occupied name in
two ways: a signing nonce can collide with one of the $q_h$ names the
adversary submitted ($q_s q_h$ pairs), or two signing nonces can
collide with each other ($q_s(q_s-1)/2$ pairs). Each pair collides with
probability exactly $2^{-320}$, so a union bound over all pairs gives
\[
 \Pr[\text{some collision}]\ \leq\
 \frac{q_s q_h+q_s(q_s-1)/2}{2^{320}},
\]
truncated at $1$; the subtraction $q_s-1$ is truncated natural
subtraction, as in the formal declaration. The stopped-run advantage
itself is unchanged on collision-free runs, hence
\[
 \Adv_{\mathrm{EUF}}(\beta,\mu_K,A)
 \ \leq\ \Pr[\text{stopped run wins}]+\epscoll.
\]
This is a name-table collision loss of the classical game; it is not a
QROM statement, and the $320$-bit nonce accounting is not a security
level.\src{C04,C05,C18}

\subsection{Assembling the closed reduction}
The four steps compose directly. The adaptive composition
(Lemma~\ref{lem:composition}) bounds the stopped run by
$\Phi((1+e)^{q_s}-1,b)$ through the event transfer of
Section~\ref{sec:direction}; the collision removal
(Section~\ref{sec:collstop}) adds $\epscoll$; and the extraction
(Section~\ref{sec:extraction}) identifies the remaining term with
$b=\Adv_{\mathrm{MT}}(q_h+1,\mu_H,B_A)$ for the concrete reducer
$B_A=\code{Reduction.build}\ \beta\ A\ S$. Together these are
\eqref{eq:conditional}, with the overall clipping at $1$.
The second moment controls a density ratio over the entire observation,
not merely its expected value or a rejection indicator. Key-law
conditioning remains in $\mu_H$. The Sign constant $e$ belongs to the
local sampler comparison, and cannot be replaced by a measured KeyGen
acceptance rate or by the separately reported centering-error number.
\src{C01,C04,C06}
